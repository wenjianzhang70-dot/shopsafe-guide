Gift Card Scams & Fake Delivery Texts: Holiday 2026

Updated October 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Not every holiday scam needs a fake store. Two of the highest-volume fraud types need nothing more than your phone number: the fake delivery text and the gift card trap. AI has made both more convincing — error-free, personalized smishing at scale, per the UK's National Trading Standards 2026 watchlist.

Scam 1: "Your package has a problem" (smishing)

You get a text — apparently from UPS, FedEx, USPS, or Amazon — about a delivery problem. The link asks you to "verify" an address or payment. It harvests credentials or installs malware. The FTC reports hundreds of millions in annual consumer losses tied to text-message scams alone.

Red flags: unexpected "verify delivery" requests; shortened URLs or links that don't match the carrier's real domain; any request for passwords or payment info.

The 30-second habit: never tap the link. Track the package from the store's official site or app instead. Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM).

Scam 2: Drained and tampered gift cards

Fraudsters tamper with gift cards on store racks (recording the numbers, waiting for activation, then draining them) or push fake "free gift card" offers online that harvest personal data first. Some drained cards are emptied within minutes of activation.

The 30-second habit: buy gift cards from a cashier or directly from the retailer — never off an unattended rack. Register the card online immediately after purchase so you can check the balance and report theft fast.

Scam 3: "Pay with gift cards" demands

Any caller, texter, or "support agent" who demands payment in gift cards is a scammer — full stop. No legitimate business, bank, or government agency takes gift cards as payment. The FTC's rule of thumb: if a deal seems suspicious and they want gift cards, wire transfers, or payment apps, walk away.

Scam 4: AI-written phishing at scale

The 2026 upgrade: generative AI produces error-free, personalized phishing emails and texts by the thousand — no more broken English to tip you off. INTERPOL's October 2026 campaign notes that AI is "wiping out the classic signs of a scam." The defense moves from spotting tells to verifying through official channels, every time.

Three habits for the whole season:
1. Track packages only from official sites/apps.
2. Buy gift cards from a cashier; register them immediately.
3. Gift-card payment demanded = scam, always. Hang up.
