Black Friday Scams 2026: AI-Powered Fakes to Watch For

Updated October 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Every major sale event now comes with a shadow event: the scam surge. Data from Check Point, McAfee, and Malwarebytes shows AI-built fake shops and brand impersonation scams surging around Prime Day-style events — with impersonation reports spiking roughly 50% during the event itself. Fraud teams report that fake-shop domains start spiking weeks before the sale, not on the day.

Black Friday 2026 (November 27) is five weeks away. Here's what's coming and how to shop it safely.

Scam 1: AI-built fake storefronts

The headline threat. Scammers use generative AI to spin up polished stores — realistic product photos, plausible reviews, professional layouts — in under an hour, then promote them through social media ads with "too-good-to-be-true" Black Friday prices. You pay; nothing arrives. See our 7-check guide for the full verification routine.

Scam 2: Fake ads on social feeds

A Reuters investigation found a meaningful share of scam ads running on major social platforms — some analyses suggest scammers buy premium ad placement, not just lurk in comments. During Black Friday, assume any too-good deal in your feed is guilty until proven innocent: go to the retailer's official site directly instead of clicking the ad.

Scam 3: "Order problem" phishing

Texts and emails claiming a delivery problem or a payment issue with your Black Friday order, linking to a credential-harvesting page. Rule: track orders from the store's official site or app — never from a link in a message.

Scam 4: Fake coupon and deal-finder apps

Seasonal "deal finder" and coupon apps spike every November; some carry malicious code. Download apps only from official app stores, never from links in texts or social posts — and check permissions (a coupon app has no business reading your contacts). Delete holiday-only apps when the season ends.

The Black Friday safety checklist

Before you buy:
1. Go to the retailer's official site directly — never via an ad or message link.
2. Search "[store name] + scam/reviews."
3. Pay by credit card (chargeback protection); never by gift card, wire, or payment app on an unfamiliar store.
4. Screenshot the product page and your confirmation — it makes disputes far easier.

After you buy: track from the official app; ignore "delivery problem" texts unless verified.

Why 2026 is different

Two shifts make this year harder than last: first, AI removed the classic tells — no more typos, no more clumsy layouts, as INTERPOL noted in its October 2026 deepfake-scam campaign. Second, AI shopping assistants that browse and buy on your behalf are going mainstream, and security researchers warn they can be tricked by the same fake stores. Canada's cyber centre advises staying actively involved in purchase decisions rather than delegating them to AI.
