How to Spot AI-Generated Fake Online Stores: 7 Checks (2026)

Updated October 19, 2026 · 7 min read

In October 2026, Canada's Centre for Cyber Security warned holiday shoppers that AI is making classic shopping scams dramatically easier: convincing fake storefronts, polished AI-generated reviews, professional-looking ads. Days later, the UK's National Trading Standards published its first-ever AI scam watchlist.

The unsettling part: reports show AI can now generate a convincing fake e-commerce site — product descriptions, reviews, the works — in under an hour. The old advice ("look for typos") is dead; AI doesn't make typos. Here's what actually works now.

Check 1: Read the URL like a detective

Real brands use clean URLs: dyson.com, ugg.com. Scam sites use look-alikes: extra words (dyson-outlet-sale), dashes, misspellings, or odd endings like .shop, .biz, .info. Type the retailer's address directly into your browser instead of clicking a link — and never trust autofill on an unfamiliar domain.

Check 2: Look for real contact options

Legitimate stores list a customer service number, a physical address, or live chat. Scam sites typically offer only a web form — or a Gmail/Yahoo address — or nothing at all. No real-world accountability means no real business.

Check 3: Search the name + "scam"

Before buying, search the retailer's name along with "reviews," "scam," or "complaints." This single step, recommended by the FTC, catches a large share of fake stores — victims and watchdogs usually post warnings within days of a scam site appearing.

Check 4: Read 3 reviews — not 300

Ignore the five-star wall; AI can generate hundreds of glowing reviews. Instead read a few in detail and look for: real photos, mentions of shipping/packaging/returns, and human language. Emoji storms and generic praise ("great product, fast shipping!!!") are cheap to fake.

Check 5: Check the domain age

Paste the URL into a WHOIS lookup. If the domain was registered within the last few months, treat it as a pop-up scam until proven otherwise — fraud teams report that fake-shop domains spike weeks before major sale events, then vanish after the holidays.

Check 6: Don't trust the padlock alone

HTTPS and the padlock icon only mean the connection is encrypted. Scam sites obtain certificates in minutes. Necessary — but no longer sufficient. One consumer tech outlet called this "the end of the HTTPS checklist era."

Check 7: Ask AI to check it for you

Paste the link into ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity and ask whether the store looks legitimate and why. AI assistants are decent at spotting the patterns above — but treat the answer as guidance, not a verdict. Canada's cyber centre puts it well: AI tools "cannot reliably determine whether a seller is trustworthy in the same way a person can."

The 2-minute routine: URL check → contact page → "name + scam" search → domain age. Four steps, two minutes, catches most AI-built fakes.

If you already paid a fake store

  1. Paid by credit card? Contact your issuer immediately — chargeback protections are strongest here. This is why consumer agencies say: never pay a suspicious store by gift card, wire transfer, or payment app.
  2. Saved your card on the site? Ask your bank for a replacement card; assume the number is compromised.
  3. Report it: in the US, the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov; forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM).
Prime Day / Black Friday warning: impersonation reports spike ~50% during major sale events. Treat AI-built fake storefronts as the default threat during sales — not the exception.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can you tell if an online store is fake?

Check the URL for misspellings or odd extensions, look for real contact information, search the store name plus "scam," be wary of far-below-market prices, and check the domain age.

Can AI create fake online stores?

Yes — in under an hour, with product descriptions and reviews. Both Canadian and UK authorities warned about AI-built fake storefronts in October 2026.

Is it safe to buy from a site with HTTPS?

Not necessarily. Scam sites easily obtain certificates. Treat the padlock as necessary but not sufficient.
